    What is a Forecast Bust?

    Forecast Bust: A statistically significant forecast error versus the historical error distribution for that variable, region, and lead time — not an arbitrary millimetre cutoff.

    Forecast Error = Forecast − Reference / verification

    Error above the chosen historical percentile → Potential Forecast Bust

    Methodology

    How Clime turns an existing forecast into a reliability assessment.

    1. Ingest NWP forecast (Day 1–10)
    ↓
    2. Historical forecast + reference → error-pattern database
    ↓
    3. Statistical bust thresholds (variable × region × lead time)
    ↓
    4. Calibrated Random Forest bust-probability model
    ↓
    5. Confidence map (1 − bust probability)
    ↓
    6. Feature Attribution — explain factors contributing to bust risk
    ↓
    7. Operational dashboard + integration API

    Data leakage safeguard

    The model is trained only on variables known at forecast time: NWP precipitation, temperature, pressure, humidity, wind, lead time, location, spatial gradient, pressure variation, and historical error statistics. Observed/reference weather is used only to derive the bust label offline. Clime does not generate another weather forecast.

    Positioning

    Existing weather models tell us what the weather may be. Clime estimates how reliable those forecasts are and where they may experience unusually large errors. Production systems can replace synthetic demonstration pairs with operational forecast/reference data using the same schema. Clime does not replace official meteorological forecasts.

    Integration API

    Same-origin JSON endpoints for other systems. This layer returns reliability, not a replacement weather forecast.

    About Clime

    Don't just forecast the weather. Forecast the reliability of the forecast.

    Clime addresses the gap between weather prediction and forecast reliability by identifying regions and lead times where medium-range NWP forecasts are likely to experience significant errors.

    Existing weather models tell us what the weather may be. Clime predicts how reliable those forecasts are and where they may experience unusually large errors.

    Clime does not replace NWP forecasting; it predicts where and when an existing forecast may experience unusually large errors.

    What it provides

    • Regional confidence estimates
    • Day 1–Day 10 reliability tracking
    • Bust probability scoring
    • Error-prone area identification
    • Explainable model factors
    • Historical event replay

    What it does not provide

    • Guaranteed forecast failure detection
    • Replacement for official meteorological forecasts
    • Causal meteorological explanations
